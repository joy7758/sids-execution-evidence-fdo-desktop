## 4. Related Work

Digital-object work provides the broadest background for this paper. The digital object lineage emphasizes persistent object identity, typed object interaction, and machine-handled services rather than unstructured runtime records (Kahn and Wilensky, 2006; DONA Foundation, 2018). More recent FDO discussions continue that object-centered view while operating at a much broader systems level than the present paper (Soiland-Reyes et al., 2024). That background matters here because the current profile is also object-centered. At the same time, this paper does not propose a new object architecture, a new protocol layer, or a full FDO interoperability stack. Its scope is much smaller: one accountability object for one operation.

The closest methodological comparison comes from provenance and validity work. PROV-DM establishes a general provenance model for entities, activities, and agents, while PROV-Constraints makes explicit that provenance representations can also be subject to validity and constraint-checking conditions (Moreau and Missier, 2013; Cheney et al., 2013). This paper is aligned with that spirit, especially in treating explicit consistency conditions as first-class parts of the artifact. However, it does not attempt to be a general provenance family or a full interchange model. The present profile uses provenance as one bounded component inside a larger accountability object that also includes policy, evidence, and validation metadata.

Logs, traces, and audit trails form another adjacent line. They are essential for runtime observability, debugging, and operational review, but they are not automatically the same as a compact accountability object for one operation. The distinction made in this paper is therefore a scope distinction rather than a rejection of logging. Logging remains useful. The present claim is only that a reviewer-facing accountability artifact may need a smaller and more explicitly checkable surface than a raw trace transcript. A short related-work treatment is sufficient here because the paper does not benchmark logging systems or propose a replacement telemetry stack. It isolates a different review target: independently checkable operation statements.

Finally, the paper is close to artifact-oriented verification and reproducibility norms. Adjacent lines such as in-toto and SLSA show that software artifacts can carry explicit verification surfaces and downstream provenance expectations (Torres-Arias et al., 2019; [citation pending]). The artifact-review perspective is also relevant because the present contribution is meant to be documented, exercisable, and inspectable as a small research object rather than as a large evaluation platform (Association for Computing Machinery, 2020). That is why the current paper keeps its claims narrow. The value is not breadth. The value is that the profile, validator, examples, and demo form a small closure that reviewers can inspect directly.
