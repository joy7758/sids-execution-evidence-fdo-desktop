Digital-object work provides the overall context. The digital object lineage emphasizes persistent identity and machine-handled interaction, while DOIP makes that interaction explicit at protocol level [@kahn2006framework; @dona2018doip]. Recent FDO work studies those ideas at a larger systems scale than the present paper [@soilandreyes2024evaluating]. That larger lineage matters here because the present paper borrows the object-centered setting but not the larger scope.

Provenance work is the closest conceptual neighbor. PROV-DM defines general models for entities, activities, and agents, and PROV-Constraints makes explicit that provenance representations can carry validity conditions [@groth2013provoverview; @moreau2013provdm; @cheney2013provconstraints]. The present paper shares the emphasis on explicit relations and checkable consistency, but narrows the target to one operation-centered object.

Logs and traces remain essential for observability and debugging. The present paper does not compete with telemetry systems. It isolates a different review target: a compact artifact that a third party can check after one operation rather than a larger runtime transcript. The distinction is therefore about review target, not about replacing existing trace surfaces. That narrower target is what allows the paper to stay inspectable within workshop scale.

Artifact-oriented verification offers another useful comparison point. in-toto shows how explicit step records can support downstream verification, and ACM artifact-review guidance emphasizes inspectable, exercisable research objects [@torresarias2019intoto; @acm2020artifactbadging]. The present paper adopts that smaller artifact posture for one operation accountability object and keeps the contribution reviewable at workshop scale.
